Seven hues of similar chroma, ordered so neighbours differ in lightness as well as hue — an asset class stays readable to someone who cannot separate red from green, which §2's rule already requires of every gain and loss.

Depth — three surfaces, not one
Ordinary card
Flat surface, hairline border, no shadow. Most of the app.
Hero surface
Corner-lit gradient, lifted shadow, 1px top highlight. One per screen.
Inset
Recessed, darker than its parent. Caveats, totals, segmented tracks.

This is the rule §4 already states — "not everything is a card" — given something to spend. The gradient is a corner light, not a wash: it never crosses a figure, and tabular numerals always sit on flat ground.

Warmth — a tile per asset class
Mutual funds Bonds US equity Property

A tinted tile and a stroke icon per class, drawn from the same ramp, so a row carries its identity at a glance instead of through an 8px dot. Stroke SVG inline — no icon font, no new dependency.

Editable row — a row that is also a form

The spending plan, the deposit terms, the loan form: a record and the inputs that change it, on one line. Nothing else in the kit covers it, and it is the densest control pattern in the app.

Groceries Compulsory

Inset ground. The row recedes below its card, so a column of them reads as a list rather than as nine cards.

Mono in the inputs. Figures a person types and compares down a column — the one place outside a table the rule keeps it.

Icon for the destructive one. "Archive" as a text link per row is how Holdings reached forty of them. 34px hits the 44px target with its padding.

At phone width the inputs wrap under the name and the archive button stays on the first line — a 92px field beside a 150px name does not fit in 366px, and two fields certainly do not.
