Family Finance Buddy · app icon

Three marks, one household

All three are built from the rupee sign's most distinctive feature — its two horizontal bars — because that is the one shape that says "money, in India" with no words and no flag. Each is drawn on a 1024 grid, tested down to 16 pixels, and rendered here at the sizes that actually matter: the home screen, the tab bar, the browser tab.

The ledger rule

What it is. The rupee's two bars, drawn as ledger rules — then a third, shorter line beneath them with a dot at its end. That third line is the entry you just made, and the dot is where it lands.

Why it works small. Three horizontal strokes and a circle is about the least a mark can be while still being distinctive. There is no counter to fill in, no thin stem to disappear, no letterform to misread. At 16 pixels it is still three lines and a dot.

Why it fits this app. The whole product is a ledger that computes. Two bars are the money; the shorter teal line is the record; the dot is the resolved figure. It reads as ordered rather than as growth-arrow optimism, which suits an app whose main virtue is that its arithmetic is correct.

What it is. A roofline over the same two bars — the household above, the money below. The roof is drawn as a chevron rather than a filled house, so it stays a shape rather than becoming a property-listing icon.

Why it might win. This is the only one of the three that says family, which is the actual point of the product. The name is Family Finance Buddy, not Finance Ledger.

The risk. Roof plus money is close to territory occupied by home-loan and property apps, and at 16 pixels the chevron thickens into a triangle. It is the warmest of the three and the least distinctive.

What it is. A widening curve rising beneath the two bars — compounding drawn as a shape rather than as a chart with an arrowhead on it. The second bar is held back in opacity so the eye reads the curve first.

Why it might win. It is the most energetic of the three, and the curve is genuinely distinctive — a spiral segment is rarer than a rising line.

The risk. At 16 pixels the curve and the bars crowd into one another and the faded bar drops out entirely, leaving a shape that could be almost anything. It also drifts close to reading as a letterform in some scripts. It photographs best of the three at large sizes and survives worst at small ones — the opposite of what an app icon is for.

Recommendation

Concept 01, the ledger rule. It is the only one that loses nothing between 1024 and 16 pixels, it carries the brass and teal already used throughout the app so the icon and the interface read as one thing, and its meaning is the product's actual promise: your money on the top two lines, and a correct entry beneath them. Concept 02 is the one to revisit if the app is ever positioned to other families rather than used by yours, because warmth matters more in a store listing than it does on your own home screen.

Seen in place

Concept 01 on a home screen, in a tab bar, and as a favicon — the three contexts where an icon actually has to work.

Production notes

ItemSpec
MasterSingle SVG on a 1024 × 1024 grid, no gradients, no strokes that scale badly, four flat fills. Everything else is generated from it.
Safe areaArtwork sits inside the central 80%. Android adaptive icons and watchOS both crop to a circle — the bars must not touch the edge.
AndroidAdaptive icon: the dark plate as the background layer, the bars and dot as the foreground layer, so the system's motion and masking work properly. Plus a monochrome layer for themed icons.
iOS1024 square, no transparency, no pre-applied corner radius — the system rounds it.
FaviconSVG favicon with a 32 px PNG fallback. The mark needs no simplification at that size, which was the point of choosing it.
ColoursPlate #151B24 · bars #D9AC4C · entry line #0D7466 · dot #48BBA6. The same brass and teal as the interface.
MonochromeAll four elements in a single ink for themed icons, notification badges and print. It survives, because the mark is shape rather than colour.
Don'tNo gradient, no drop shadow, no ₹ glyph rendered as type (it changes shape with every font), no name inside the tile.
